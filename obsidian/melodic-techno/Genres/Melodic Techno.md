# Melodic Techno

## Definition
Melodic techno emphasizes harmony, emotional atmosphere, and long-form cinematic builds over the raw, industrial repetition of classic techno. <cite index="8-1">Compared to standard techno, melodic techno places a greater emphasis on harmony and melody, running slightly slower — roughly 120-128 BPM versus the 125-135 BPM of harder, more minimal techno — and layering in lush synth lines for a more introspective, emotional atmosphere.</cite>

Texturally, the genre relies on complex layering — organic elements like <cite index="2-1">wind chimes, percussion, and field recordings</cite> combined with wide synth pads to build an immersive "journey" arc across a track rather than a flat loop.

## History
<cite index="8-1">The style split off from harder, drop-oriented techno in the late 2010s</cite>, with **Tale of Us** and **Stephan Bodzin** widely credited as the artists who pushed the sound toward more melodic content and emotional depth, and **Afterlife** and **Innervisions** as the labels that gave the style its early institutional home.

## Defining Characteristics
- Tempo: 120–128 BPM (slower than harder techno strains)
- Long, cinematic builds and breakdowns — often 32+ bars
- Layered organic textures over synthetic elements (see [[Layering & Sound Design]])
- Emotional/minor-key melodic content, frequently arpeggiated
- Strong visual/audiovisual identity at the label level (Afterlife's production design is part of the genre's cultural footprint)
