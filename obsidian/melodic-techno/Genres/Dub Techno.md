# Dub Techno

## Definition
A blend of minimal techno's repetition with Jamaican dub reggae's mixing philosophy — deep basslines, spacious echo, and reverberant chords over restrained, often four-chord loops.

## History
<cite index="17-1">The genre coalesced in early-1990s Berlin around Basic Channel and related labels, drawing on Detroit techno's futurism and Jamaican dub's mixing philosophy while embodying the austere spatial aesthetics of the Berlin scene.</cite> <cite index="15-1">Mark Ernestus and Moritz von Oswald, working as Basic Channel, developed the dub techno sound by blending deep basslines with minimalistic rhythms</cite>, releasing tracks like "Phylyps Trak" that became genre-defining.

<cite index="17-1">Later artists including Porter Ricks, Fluxion, Vainqueur, and Vladislav Delay expanded the palette, while Rhythm & Sound brought an overt reggae/dub sensibility into the techno framework. In the 2000s the sound globalized, with Deepchord and Echospace reconnecting it to Detroit's lineage, and labels such as Echocord, Styrax, and Modern Love fostering a further wave of atmospheric releases.</cite>

## Defining Characteristics
- Tempo: roughly 115–130 BPM
- <cite index="13-1">Extensive reverb and delay used to build deep, immersive sonic environments</cite>
- Minimal, low-register melodic content — darkness through restraint rather than harmonic complexity
- Every element given significant "breathing space" in the mix — a hallmark organic, spacious feel (per genre reference sources)

## Notes
<cite index="16-1">Dub techno's echo-and-sub-bass approach also shaped early-2000s UK dubstep — Burial's 2006 album Untrue drew directly on Basic Channel's minimalism</cite>, and in the 2010s the same delay/reverb techniques fed into warmer, groove-oriented deep house production.
