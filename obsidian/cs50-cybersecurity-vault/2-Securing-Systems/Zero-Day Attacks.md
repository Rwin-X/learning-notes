# Zero-Day Attacks

An attack exploiting a vulnerability that the software vendor doesn't yet
know about (or knows about but hasn't patched) — "zero days" since the
fix was made available, because there is no fix yet. No signature exists
for [[Antivirus]] to catch it, and [[Automatic Updates]] can't help
before a patch exists.

High value in cybercrime and state-sponsored attacks specifically because
they bypass the standard defenses that work against known threats.
Discovery and disclosure connects to [[Bug Bounty]] programs and the
[[Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures CVE]] system in Week 3 — the
moment a zero-day is responsibly disclosed and patched, it stops being a
zero-day and becomes a tracked, patchable vulnerability instead.
