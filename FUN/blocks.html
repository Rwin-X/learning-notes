Blocks

Encrypt the same picture with AES in three modes. In one of them the picture survives the encryption.

Same key, same picture

A block is 16 bytes, which here is 16 pixels in a row. The count is how many different 16-byte blocks each image contains.

Now tamper with the ciphertext

Click any of the three encrypted images to flip one bit at that spot, then decrypt with the right key. The marked square shows where the bit was flipped, and every byte that ends up different from the original is drawn in blue.

Nothing here notices the damage. Encryption alone hides content but does not protect it from change, which is why real systems use an authenticated mode such as GCM.
